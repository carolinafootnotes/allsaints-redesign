# Sermons archive — what's the right home on the new site?

## The question (one sentence)

When someone wants to hear or read a past sermon, where should they go on the new website?

## Why we're asking

The current site has a "Get the Latest" section that includes some sermon content alongside bulletins, the Weekly Word, and annual reports. The new site needs a clear home for sermons. The design team gave three different opinions, and you should decide which one fits the parish best.

## The three options

### A. Build a dedicated sermons archive page

A new page on the site listing recent sermons in chronological order. Each entry has the preacher's name, the date, the Scripture, and an embedded video or audio player. New sermons get added weekly.

**Pros:** Best for someone Googling "Episcopal church Concord sermon." Owns the parish's preaching voice on our own domain. Highest signal for clergy candidates considering this call (they read sermons to evaluate theological voice).

**Cons:** Real ongoing maintenance — Nate or someone has to add each new sermon every week. A sermons page that's months out of date reads worse than no page at all.

### B. No dedicated page — just link out to YouTube and the podcast

Watch & Listen page gets a small "Recent Sermons" link that goes straight to the YouTube channel or podcast feed. We don't host any sermon content directly on the new site.

**Pros:** Zero maintenance. YouTube and the podcast already organize sermons well. Honest about where the active sermon library lives.

**Cons:** Visitors leave our site to listen. We don't capture the Google search traffic for sermons. Clergy candidates have to hunt for sermons rather than seeing them as part of the parish's online portfolio.

### C. Lightweight middle: show the last 5–6 sermons on Watch & Listen, no full archive

The Watch & Listen page lists the most recent 5 or 6 sermons with title, date, and an embedded player. Older sermons drop off the bottom — anyone wanting older content goes to YouTube or the podcast. Same effort to add a new sermon as Option B, plus one quick swap to push the oldest off the list.

**Pros:** Captures most of the "what is preached here" signal without a full archive's maintenance load. Looks intentional and current. Easy weekly task.

**Cons:** Older sermons aren't searchable on the parish site. The cutoff (5 or 6 sermons) is arbitrary.

## What we'd like from you

One of:
1. "Build the full archive (A) — sermons matter and we'll keep it current."
2. "Just link out (B) — let YouTube and the podcast do the work."
3. "The 5–6 recent list (C) — best of both."
4. "Something else: [your idea]"

If you have a strong view on who would commit to keeping a sermons page updated weekly, please share that too — it changes the math on Option A.
