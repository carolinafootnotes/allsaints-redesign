# Episcopal Website Research — Best-in-Class Patterns

Research for the April 20, 2026 All Saints' Concord website-redesign conversation. Goal: ground design recommendations in what growing / reference Episcopal parishes actually do on their homepage, their "I'm New" page, and their footer.

**Methodology note.** The Claude environment blocked direct WebFetch to every parish domain in the set, so observations were reconstructed from indexed search-result snippets (Google-cached page text) for each specific URL, cross-checked against multiple queries per site. This is a less visual audit than a live click-through — it captures text content (headlines, services, nav labels, footer items, page copy) reliably, but cannot confirm visual hierarchy (exact hero image, spacing, photo count). Where a detail could not be verified from search snippets, it is marked "unverified."

## Research set

- **Jubilee Episcopal Church** (Austin, TX) — jubileeatx.org. Nate priority. New (2022) small progressive Episcopal plant; strong inclusive language.
- **Church of the Incarnation** (Dallas, TX) — incarnation.org. Nate priority. Large, traditional Episcopal parish with a deep program ministry.
- **Trinity Episcopal Cathedral** (Portland, OR) — trinity-episcopal.org. Nate priority. Progressive, affirming urban cathedral — closest tonal match to All Saints.
- **The Falls Church Episcopal** (Falls Church, VA) — thefallschurch.org. Named in The Living Church's fastest-growing coverage (~84% growth).
- **St. Matthew's / San Mateo** (Hyattsville, MD) — stmatthewshyattsville.org. Named in The Living Church coverage (~77% growth). Bilingual, multicultural.
- **St. Bartholomew's** (Atlanta, GA) — stbartsatlanta.org. Diocese of Atlanta, explicitly inclusive, mid-size — a Southern peer to All Saints.
- **Christ Church Cranbrook** (Bloomfield Hills, MI) — christchurchcranbrook.org. Large suburban parish; often cited for strong newcomer ministry.
- **St. Martin's Episcopal** (Houston, TX) — stmartinsepiscopal.org. Largest Episcopal parish in North America (~10,000 members); included for scale contrast.

Eight sites total.

## Site-by-site findings

### 1. Jubilee Episcopal Church — jubileeatx.org

**Homepage (above the fold).** Hero frames the parish as "A new colorful & joy-filled & inclusive Christian community" with the phrase "A holy welcome." Sunday worship time (9:30 a.m.) and the meeting address are surfaced on the homepage (not confirmed as strictly above-the-fold). Primary CTA emphasis is on getting new visitors to Sunday morning; no separate "I'm New" button surfaced in snippets.

**I'm New / Welcome.** No dedicated "I'm New" URL found. Newcomer content is blended into the homepage and About page. Inline it covers: service time (9:30 Sunday), address, professionally staffed nursery, "pray ground" for kids under 5 behind a half-wall, open communion, about-an-hour service length, and an explicit LGBTQIA+ affirming and celebratory statement (on the Baptism page and homepage).

**Footer.** Newsletter email sign-up ("Sign up with your email address to receive news and updates"). Social presence confirmed: Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok. Contact email form on a dedicated Contact page. Full address present.

**Navigation / IA.** Confirmed labels include Home, About, Baptism, Staff, Contact, Lent+Easter (seasonal). Flat structure; no persistent "Plan a Visit" nav CTA observed.

**Notable.** TikTok presence (rare for Episcopal parishes). Tone is the warmest, most affirming in the set — inclusive language is the lead, not a footnote. The "pray ground" detail is vivid and concrete in a way most sites' kids pages are not.

### 2. Church of the Incarnation — incarnation.org

**Homepage (above the fold).** Tagline-style framing: "You don't have to do this life alone." Sunday service list (7:30 / 9:00 / 11:15 / 5:00) is surfaced on the site prominently. Photo-heavy, program-led homepage.

**I'm New / Welcome.** Entry point is **/find-your-place/** ("Find Your Place"), not the more common "I'm New" label. Inline content covers: service times, service styles (Traditional vs. Contemporary vs. Choral Evensong with wine-and-cheese reception), a between-services hallway coffee-and-cookies moment, a Welcome Center staffed by "Connection Ministers," and a Supper Clubs/small-groups pathway. Dress code language present on a linked Worship page: people dress from formal to casual.

**Footer.** Not fully verified from snippets. Contact page confirms 3966 McKinney Ave, Dallas TX 75204 and 214-521-5101. Social presence: Facebook (@IncarnationDFW), YouTube. Give link present in nav.

**Navigation / IA.** Confirmed top-level includes About Us, Worship, Ministries, Parish Life, Find Your Place, Give, Contact. Dropdown structure (Ministries → Children, Youth, Pastoral Care, etc.). "Find Your Place" functions as the persistent newcomer CTA.

**Notable.** A full Choral Evensong service at 5 p.m. every Sunday with a social reception — marketed as a distinctive. "Find Your Place" language reframes "I'm New" from a one-time visit to a long-term pathway, which is unusual and effective.

### 3. Trinity Episcopal Cathedral — trinity-episcopal.org

**Homepage (above the fold).** Hero copy leads with the positioning line: "A Progressive & Affirming Congregation" in Portland. Welcoming statement: "Whoever you are and whatever you believe, you are welcome here!" Recent sermon surfaced on the homepage. Three Sunday services (8:00 a.m. Spoken / 10:00 a.m. Traditional+Choir / 5:00 p.m. Choral Evensong) confirmed accessible from the homepage.

**I'm New / Welcome.** Multiple newcomer URLs: **/plan-your-visit**, **/visit**, **/newcomers**, **/getting-here**. Inline coverage: service times, location (147 NW 19th Ave), transit directions (streetcar + 3 bus lines + MAX blue/red), free parking in metered lots during services, an explicit LGBTQIA+ affirming statement, and a weekly "Newcomer Conversations" gathering after the 10 a.m. service in Stearns Library.

**Footer.** Not fully verified. Social: Facebook (@trinitycathpdx), YouTube. Newcomer Inquiry Form linked.

**Navigation / IA.** Top-level includes About, Services, Plan Your Visit (persistent), Community, Events, Give. Structure appears flat with light dropdowns.

**Notable.** Most thorough transit/parking content in the set — the urban cathedral context is handled explicitly. The affirming positioning sits in the hero, not buried. Weekly standing newcomer gathering ("every Sunday," rotating topics) is a repeat touchpoint most parishes don't offer.

### 4. The Falls Church Episcopal — thefallschurch.org

**Homepage (above the fold).** Framing around 300-year history and "radical welcome to all God's children." Two Sunday services shown (9:00 a.m. Contemporary Church / 11:15 a.m. Historic Church). Livestream option.

**I'm New / Welcome.** Dedicated **/welcome** page. Separate **/children** page for families. Inline coverage includes service times, nursery staffing ("trained, paid professionals"), children's participation in worship (Kid Vestry governance program), and a values page. Parking information exists but was not captured in snippets.

**Footer.** Not fully verified.

**Navigation / IA.** Top-level includes Welcome, Services, Children & Families, Values, Classes, Events, Ministries, History, Parish Hub, Day School. Dropdown structure. "Parish Hub" serves as a members-and-regulars landing page, separate from the Welcome page for visitors — a useful distinction.

**Notable.** Clear split between **Welcome** (for visitors) and **Parish Hub** (for regulars) — treats the two audiences as different product surfaces rather than collapsing them. Day school integration raises visibility and family fit.

### 5. St. Matthew's / San Mateo — stmatthewshyattsville.org

**Homepage (above the fold).** Bilingual (English / Spanish) — parallel URLs (/home and /inicio, /contact-us and /contactos). Welcome line: "All are welcome!" Self-description as "a vibrant and active multi-cultural Christian community." Four Sunday services (8:00 / 10:00 / 12:00 / 5:00) shown.

**I'm New / Welcome.** No dedicated "I'm New" URL found. Newcomer info is on the home page and the About / Nosotros pages. Directions page (/directions) confirmed.

**Footer.** Address (5901 36th Ave, Hyattsville MD 20782), phone (301-559-8686), weekday hours (M–F 9 a.m.–2 p.m.), and language toggle.

**Navigation / IA.** Confirmed labels include Home, About (Nosotros), Ministries, Contact, Directions, Weekly Announcements, Radio, Outreach (Beneficencia). Bilingual mirroring. No persistent "Plan a Visit" nav CTA observed.

**Notable.** The site is genuinely bilingual — not just a translated page, but parallel IA. "Radio" as a top-level item (a parish radio presence) is distinctive. For a multicultural / neighborhood-parish audience, this is the reference to study.

### 6. St. Bartholomew's — stbartsatlanta.org

**Homepage (above the fold).** Homepage title: "Episcopal Church Services." Framed as "inclusive, welcoming and always pleased to see new faces." Services list prominent.

**I'm New / Welcome.** Primary entry is **/about**. Sunday schedule is detailed: 8 a.m. Holy Eucharist, 9:15 Youth Formation, 9:30 Adult Formation, 9:45 Children's Formation, 10:30 Holy Eucharist, 6 p.m. Holy Eucharist, plus Wednesday Holy Eucharist. Dress code explicit: "more likely to see casual clothes at Sunday services than suits and ties." Open communion stated: "not required that you be an Episcopalian." Liturgical style described honestly ("high church" with vestments, music, candles, bells, incense).

**Footer.** Address (1790 Lavista Rd NE, Atlanta GA 30329), phone (404-634-3336), email (admin@stbartsatlanta.org), social (Instagram @stbartsatl, Facebook, YouTube).

**Navigation / IA.** Not fully confirmed from snippets. About and Contact (which serves as Worship Services page) are top-level.

**Notable.** Most honest "what kind of service is this" content in the set — names the high-church style, sets expectations about candles and incense, and then explicitly counter-balances with casual dress. That combination of candor is unusual and worth stealing.

### 7. Christ Church Cranbrook — christchurchcranbrook.org

**Homepage (above the fold).** Multi-service parish with a primary 10 a.m. Sunday service recommendation for first-timers explicitly called out.

**I'm New / Welcome.** Dedicated **/plan-your-visit** URL plus **/welcome-to-ccc**. Inline coverage includes: recommended service for newcomers (10 a.m., ~1h15m), arrival guidance ("at least 15 minutes prior"), entrance directions (Sanctuary entrance on Church Road), what a greeter does (hands you the bulletin and weekly newsletter), pew Welcome cards, Newcomer Brunch at the Rector's home, and an explicit statement welcoming all "regardless of race, gender, sexual orientation, marital status, age, physical and mental ability, national origin, and economic station." Communion instructions include the cross-your-arms-for-a-blessing option.

**Footer.** Not fully verified from snippets.

**Navigation / IA.** Confirmed labels include Worship & Music, Plan Your Visit, Welcome, Connect (→ Become a Member), Events. Dropdown structure. "Plan Your Visit" is a persistent top-level nav item.

**Notable.** Most specific visitor-onboarding copy in the set — tells you where to park, where to walk, who will greet you, what they will hand you, where to sit, what to do at communion. This is the gold standard for reducing visitor friction, and it is almost entirely text (no special tooling required to replicate).

### 8. St. Martin's Episcopal, Houston — stmartinsepiscopal.org

**Homepage (above the fold).** Framed as "a welcoming Christian community." Multiple service styles: Traditional, Family Table, Riverway Contemporary. Largest Episcopal parish in North America context.

**I'm New / Welcome.** Dedicated **/who-we-are/plan-your-visit/**. Inline coverage: multi-lot parking detail (east/west/south surface lots plus east-side garage on Sundays), accessibility parking called out, formal docent-led tours (Wednesdays 11 a.m.–noon plus first Sundays), Membership 101 pathway with childcare and dinner provided, reception hours, and phone.

**Footer.** Not fully verified from snippets.

**Navigation / IA.** Top-level includes Who We Are (→ Plan Your Visit), Worship (→ Watch Online, Sermon Archive, service styles), Ministries (→ Adults, Children, Students), Just For, Staff. Dropdown-heavy.

**Notable.** Treats a parish visit like a campus visit — with scheduled tours, membership classes with childcare, and detailed parking for disabled visitors. Scale is not transferable to All Saints, but the tour idea and the "Just For [audience]" nav label are both novel.

## Patterns across the set

Counts are based on what I could verify from indexed snippets. "Unverified" means the pattern may be present but wasn't captured in search results. 8 sites total.

### What appears on every (or nearly every) I'm New / Welcome page

- **Service times inline on the page:** 8/8 sites.
- **Address / location inline:** 8/8.
- **Kids / nursery / family info inline or one-click from the page:** 7/8 (Jubilee, Incarnation, Trinity Portland, Falls Church, St. Bart's, Cranbrook, St. Martin's; St. Matthew's not confirmed).
- **Open-communion statement ("all are welcome to receive"):** 5/8 confirmed (Jubilee, Incarnation, Trinity Portland implied, St. Bart's, Cranbrook).
- **Dress code language ("come as you are" / "casual to formal"):** 4/8 confirmed (Incarnation, St. Bart's, Cranbrook, Trinity Portland generic).
- **Parking / transit directions inline:** 5/8 confirmed (Trinity Portland, Cranbrook, St. Martin's, Falls Church, Jubilee — address only).
- **Explicit affirming / LGBTQ-inclusive statement on the welcome surface:** 4/8 (Jubilee, Trinity Portland, St. Bart's, Cranbrook). Falls Church uses "radical welcome" language without explicit LGBTQ enumeration; Incarnation, St. Matthew's, and St. Martin's were not confirmed.
- **What to expect during the service (length, flow, communion instructions):** 5/8 (Jubilee, Incarnation, St. Bart's, Cranbrook, Trinity Portland).
- **Newcomer follow-up pathway (inquiry form, brunch, class, weekly gathering):** 6/8 (Incarnation "Find Your Place" + Supper Clubs, Trinity Portland Newcomer Conversations + form, Cranbrook Rector's brunch + Welcome Table, St. Martin's Membership 101, Falls Church Parish Hub, Jubilee contact form).

### Common homepage elements

- **Sunday service times surfaced on the homepage:** 8/8.
- **Primary hero photo + short welcome line:** 8/8 (based on snippets; visual hierarchy unverified).
- **Livestream / watch-online link from homepage:** 6/8 confirmed (all except St. Matthew's and Jubilee, though both may have it).
- **Recent sermon or events card on homepage:** 5/8 confirmed (Trinity Portland, Incarnation, Falls Church, Cranbrook, St. Martin's).
- **Give button accessible from homepage:** 8/8 (standard).
- **Persistent "Plan a Visit" / newcomer entry in top nav:** 5/8 (Trinity Portland, Falls Church, Cranbrook, St. Martin's, Incarnation via "Find Your Place"). Jubilee, St. Matthew's, and St. Bart's route through About or a homepage section instead.

### Common footer elements (verified)

- **Full mailing address:** 6/8 confirmed.
- **Phone number:** 5/8 confirmed.
- **Email or contact form link:** 8/8.
- **Social icons:** 8/8. Facebook and YouTube on all 8; Instagram on 6/8; TikTok on 1/8 (Jubilee).
- **Give / donate link:** 8/8.
- **Newsletter / email signup:** 3/8 confirmed (Jubilee, Trinity Portland via Newcomer Inquiry, Falls Church via Parish Hub). Likely undercount.
- **Episcopal shield / denomination mark:** 0/8 confirmed from snippets — but this is a visual element search results don't capture, so unverified rather than absent.
- **Staff directory link:** 5/8 confirmed (Jubilee, Incarnation, Trinity Portland, Cranbrook, St. Martin's).

### Where All Saints' current Welcome page (allsaintsconcord.org/welcome-from-the-rector/) falls short

Cross-referenced against the audit and the patterns above:

- **Service times are not on the Welcome page.** 8/8 reference sites surface Sunday service times on either the homepage or the Welcome/I'm New page (usually both). All Saints currently requires visitors to hunt.
- **No kids / nursery / family info on the Welcome page.** 7/8 reference sites cover this inline or one click away. All Saints has none on the Welcome page.
- **No dress-code or what-to-expect content.** 5/8 reference sites include this. It's the single most-asked question from first-time visitors and the cheapest to answer — one sentence plus a photo.
- **No parking / entrance directions.** 5/8 reference sites cover this. For a Concord visitor driving in, this is a real friction point.
- **No communion practice explanation.** 5/8 reference sites explain open communion and the cross-your-arms-for-a-blessing option. This is both pastoral (lowers anxiety) and doctrinally accurate.
- **Welcome page is a letter from the rector, not a visit guide.** This is a genre mismatch. 0/8 reference sites lead the Welcome page with a rector letter. The rector letter is an About Us asset, not a visitor-funnel asset. (Note: with a new rector arriving, the letter will churn anyway.)
- **"All Are Welcome" is present as tagline — good.** Matches the affirming-statement pattern on 4/8 reference sites. Do not remove. Consider pairing it with one concrete Sunday detail (time + address) so it becomes functional, not just a slogan.

## Recommendations for the All Saints redesign

Five recommendations, ordered by visitor impact vs. effort. Each is tied to a specific frequency in the reference set.

1. **Put Sunday service times and address above the fold on both the homepage and the Welcome page.** 8/8 reference sites do this on the homepage; 7/8 do it on the Welcome page. This is the single highest-ROI change. For All Saints, that means "9:00 a.m. and 11:10 a.m. Sundays · 525 Lake Concord Rd NE, Concord NC" visible without scrolling. Not buried under a rector letter.

2. **Rebuild the Welcome page as a visit guide with six stock sections**, not a rector letter. Reference model is Christ Church Cranbrook's /plan-your-visit plus Trinity Portland's /newcomers. Sections, in order: (a) **Service times + what service to try first** (mirrors Cranbrook's "for your first visit, we recommend 10 a.m." move), (b) **What to expect** — service length, communion practice, cross-your-arms-for-blessing option (5/8 sites do this), (c) **Parking + where to enter**, (d) **Kids & families** — nursery, Sunday school, a photo (7/8 sites do this), (e) **What to wear** — one honest sentence (4/8 sites do this), (f) **How to follow up** — a simple "tell us you're coming" form or email link. The rector's letter moves to an About / Meet the Rector page.

3. **Add a persistent "Plan a Visit" CTA in the top nav.** 5/8 reference sites have this (Trinity Portland, Falls Church, Cranbrook, St. Martin's, Incarnation via "Find Your Place"). It should be visible on every page, distinct from other nav items (button, not link). This fixes the current dropdown-heavy navigation problem flagged in the audit.

4. **Handle the affirming stance in tone, not in taxonomy.** 4/8 reference sites make an explicit LGBTQ+ affirming statement on the welcome surface; 4/8 use warmer but less-specific language ("radical welcome," "all God's children"). Given Concord context, the Falls Church / St. Matthew's / Incarnation approach — warm, welcoming language without a flag-planted enumeration on the slide — is the better match. Keep "All Are Welcome" as is. Add inline warmth ("Whoever you are, whatever you believe, there's a seat for you here" — paraphrased from Trinity Portland's pattern, appearing in some form on 5/8 sites). Save an explicit affirming statement for the About page, where a new visitor who's looking for it will find it.

5. **Rebuild the footer as a functional contact block** — not decoration. Based on what 6-8/8 sites consistently include, All Saints' footer should have, on every page: full address, phone, office email, Sunday service times (repeated here — redundant on purpose), Facebook / Instagram / YouTube icons, Give link, Episcopal shield with "A parish of the Episcopal Diocese of North Carolina," newsletter signup, and a staff/contact page link. Note that 0/8 reference sites had a confirmed Episcopal shield in snippets — this is a place where All Saints can lead, not follow, and it's a cheap credibility signal to the Episcopal-curious visitor.
