Since 1878

Our history

Nearly 150 years of worship, mergers, missions, and ministry in Cabarrus County. The story is still being written.

All Saints' has been part of Concord since before the town had paved streets. What started as a handful of families gathered for prayer became a parish, weathered a merger, built a campus, and sent its people out into the world.

"The Episcopal branch of the Jesus Movement, rooted in Concord since 1878."
A Walk Through the Years

Milestones along the way

Founding, building, merging, sending. Each year here is a real moment in the life of this parish.

1877

A first baptism, a first rector

On November 27, Elizabeth C. Gibson became the first Episcopalian baptized in Cabarrus County. The Rev. John Thomas Wheat, D.D., became the first rector.

1886

A first building, bought used

The young congregation bought the old Presbyterian church for $500. A modest start, and a roof of their own.

1891

Land at Cabarrus and Depot

Property at the corner of Cabarrus Avenue and Depot Street (now Spring Street) was purchased for $300. That corner would be home for the next 81 years.

1893

Officially a congregation

All Saints' was officially recognized as a congregation by the Diocese of North Carolina, formally joining the wider church.

1941

St. James joins the Diocese

A separate group of Episcopalians in Kannapolis was admitted into the Diocese as the Congregation of St. James on July 10. Two congregations, ten miles apart.

1953

St. James cornerstone

Bishop Edwin Penick presided at the cornerstone laying of St. James Church on November 7.

1954

First service in Kannapolis

The first service was held in St. James' new building on April 4.

1968

A resolution to merge

On December 8, both Concord and Kannapolis congregations read a resolution to merge. Two parishes considering becoming one.

1969

One parish, All Saints'

On January 1, the merger was voted and approved. Two churches became one parish: All Saints'.

1971

Miss Jen turns the first shovel

Groundbreaking for the present church building on April 8. Mrs. J.L. Brown, "Miss Jen," the senior communicant, turned the first shovel of dirt.

1972

A new home on Lake Concord Road

The last service in the old Spring Street building was held on June 30. The first service in the present building took place on August 6 with the placement of the Foundation Stone. Bishop Thomas A. Fraser dedicated the Church and Parish House on October 22.

1984

Governor's Volunteer Award

In June, All Saints' received the Governor's Volunteer Award for exemplary community involvement. Recognition for service that had already been quiet practice for years.

1996

Haiti CODEP begins

All Saints' began a Haiti mission with CODEP, reforestation work in the Cormeir valley, with teams traveling for years. The trips have since wound down, but the trees they helped plant remain.

2014

A preschool on campus

The Lockhart Child Development Center (LCDC) opened on the All Saints' campus, a Cabarrus County Schools partnership serving 36 pre-K students.

2026

A new interim rector

The Rev. John Johanssen began as Interim Rector on January 1, walking with the parish through its rector search and into whatever comes next.

There is a whole book of this

All Saints' kept an illustrated history of the parish: pencil drawings of the buildings, the stories behind the chandeliers and the font and the organ, and a guide to the symbolism of the windows. We have gathered it into one place, with the original book to read in full.
