Parish Profile

All Saints' Episcopal Church, Concord, North Carolina

All Saints' Episcopal Church in Concord, North Carolina, is a congregation on the move. We are a growing, diverse, and welcoming parish seeking a rector who will help us deepen discipleship, expand our impact in the community, and discern God's call for the future. Rooted in worship and shaped by relationships, we are blessed with a strong foundation: a debt-free parish, growing attendance, thriving children's and youth ministries, and a congregation eager to participate in God's mission. We seek a priest who sees possibilities where others see stability and who is excited to lead alongside a people ready for what comes.

Congregational conversations revealed a clear picture of who we are and who we aspire to become. All Saints' is a welcoming, inclusive, and outward-focused community where people are known, valued, supported, and invited to grow in faith. We seek a rector who will honor these strengths while helping us deepen our mission of "disciples making disciples for Jesus" and live more fully into our vision of "loving as God loves."

"At the heart of our hopes is a rector who leads through relationships."

Parishioners consistently emphasized the importance of presence, compassion, authenticity, wisdom, and deep listening. We seek a priest who will walk alongside people through life's joys and challenges, offering pastoral care that makes God's love tangible and strengthens our sense of community.

Above all, we seek a faithful pastor who is spiritually grounded, relationally engaged, and committed to helping us grow as disciples who make disciples. We believe God is leading All Saints' into a future marked by deeper relationships, stronger discipleship, broader community impact, and faithful growth. We seek a rector who will help us discern that future together and lead us with courage, humility, and hope.

Who We Are

Our Mission & Identity

All Saints' is rooted in the call to be a community of disciples making disciples for Jesus Christ by loving as God loves through worship, service, and presence in the world. The congregation's mission, "disciples making disciples for Jesus," and the vision, "love as God loves," are clearly reflected in parishioners' understanding of their common life. All Saints' understands itself as a church grounded in worship, active in service, and formed through authentic community in which all are welcomed and known.

All Saints' expresses its theological and spiritual identities through four core values:

Worshiping God in the Episcopal tradition

Many parishioners and external community members would describe All Saints' as a theologically center-left, progressive congregation with a broad-church liturgical style in the Episcopal tradition. In addition to regular Sunday worship services, parishioners enjoy weekly streamed Compline and Noonday Prayer, plus special services during holy days that feature other styles of worship, such as Taizé.

All Saints' parishioners approach discipleship as a lifelong journey of learning, following, and embodying the teachings of Jesus Christ in daily life. This approach involves living out the promises made in the Baptismal Covenant through continuous spiritual growth, prayer, and service. Parishioners draw deeply from scripture, historic tradition, and reason, focusing on growing in love of God, neighbor, and self.

Where We Are

Our Community

All Saints' is in the Southern Piedmont region of North Carolina, approximately a 2.5-hour drive to the mountains in the west and a 3.5-hour drive to the ocean beaches in the east. The parish is situated near the border of Concord and Kannapolis, just off Interstate 85. The two rapidly growing cities have a combined population of over 176,000, by far the largest combined suburbs of Charlotte, the nation's 14th largest city. Concord alone is ranked as the state's 10th largest city with a population over 112,000. Uptown Charlotte is a 25-mile drive, and Charlotte-Douglas International Airport is a 28-mile drive from the parish.

As Cabarrus County's population has surged, the county has become increasingly diverse. The population consists of the following racial and ethnic identities: Non-Hispanic White (55.3%), Black or African American (22.5%), Hispanic or Latino of any race (13.4%), Asian (7.8%), and other smaller groups (3.8%). The local Asian population is the county's fastest-growing demographic, expanding by roughly 34% over the last decade, followed by a 30% increase in residents identifying as multi-racial. Approximately 10.8% of the county's residents were born outside of the United States, with 16.6% of residents age five or older speaking a language other than English at home.

As the only Episcopal church in Cabarrus County, All Saints' enjoys close working relationships with several mainline churches in the immediate area, including Lutherans, Presbyterians, and Methodists. All Saints' helped establish and supports several interdenominational initiatives in areas of education, food security, health care, housing, and incarceration transition services. All Saints' has played a leading role in racial equity initiatives, led by current Senior Warden Chuck Collier. The initiative facilitated interracial and interdenominational gatherings to examine and address racial inequity candidly and constructively.

Sunday Mornings

Worship at All Saints'

All Saints' currently offers two worship services each Sunday at 9:00 a.m. and 11:10 a.m. Throughout the year, children and youth formation take place during the 10:00 a.m. hour. Adult formation takes place concurrently between services.

In response to parishioners' requests and emerging worship trends, All Saints' began offering a more casual, family service at 9:00 a.m. in the parish hall, a welcoming and relaxed space for people of all ages and especially families and children. The order of worship and liturgy are projected onto two video screens in the front of the hall. We are intentional about involving children and adults as acolytes, crucifers, lectors, and worship participants. During this service, clergy and others share lessons in a more intimate, small-group setting with children and youth at the altar before they depart for their story time during the sermon.

"Worship is the spiritual center of the parish, forming and sustaining disciples through Holy Baptism, Holy Eucharist, holy seasons, music, and the sacramental life of the Church."

A more traditional service is conducted at 11:10 a.m. in the nave. The talented Choir of All Saints' presents anthems, Psalms, and special music accompanied by the music director/organist. Occasionally, other parishioners accompany the choir on various instruments. The Bells of All Saints' perform at times during the church year and holy days. The traditional service also continues to attract new attendees.

The celebrant invites all congregants to participate in communion and explains options such as standing or kneeling, partaking of one or both elements, gluten-free hosts, or receiving a blessing only. The celebrant will take Eucharist to individuals with mobility issues. During the pandemic, All Saints' introduced the Spiritual Communion Prayer for those worshipping virtually, which continues today.

All Means All

Welcome & Belonging

All Saints' strives to live out the motto "all are welcome" in all that we do. Parishioners strive to ensure that everyone who walks through our door feels welcomed and is greeted personally. We welcome families and the joyful noise of young children by offering busy bags and a service adapted specifically for families. We welcome and affirm all members of the LGBTQ+ community in our theological beliefs and practices. All Saints' has staffed booths at the Salisbury Pride Festival and Pride Night at the Kannapolis Cannon Ballers baseball game. We embrace our staff members and parishioners who identify as LGBTQ+. Parishioners respect one another's chosen pronouns and have sought to eliminate some of the gendered references to God in our services.

All Saints' strives toward racial justice and healing. In 2017, we partnered with five area churches (two traditionally African American and three traditionally White mainline churches) to found Racial Equity Cabarrus. The initiative hosted 34 two-day workshops, many of which took place at All Saints'. Organizers trained approximately 3,400 community members over the past nine years. Our church also has explored racial justice and healing through adult formation classes and books, which are readily available in our Welcome Center's library. All Saints' has partnered with Grace Lutheran Church, a traditionally African American church in the local community, to build relationships through shared parish dinners and activities.

Concerning physical accessibility, the parking lot, nave, welcome center, parish hall, and education wing are all at ground level with no stairs, making navigation easy for those with mobility challenges. The education wing's lower level includes a community room, often used for meetings. Growing Together

Formation & Discipleship

At All Saints', we believe the Church of the future is being formed today. We are intentionally cultivating a parish where all generations worship, serve, learn, and lead together. By investing in children and youth, embracing technology as a tool for connection, empowering lay leadership, and engaging our wider community, we are building a congregation rooted in Episcopal tradition and responsive to a changing world.

Intergenerational worship is central to this vision. Every Sunday at 9:00 a.m., children participate fully in both the Liturgy of the Word and the Liturgy of the Table. Youth serve as acolytes, Gospel bearers, readers, and leaders alongside adults of all ages. We recognize that children are not simply the future of the Church; they are the Church today.

Our investment in young people extends beyond Sunday worship. All Saints' employs a full-time director of youth and family ministries and an assistant to support this growing ministry. Youth serve in outreach projects, work in church gardens, participate in mission experiences, and help lead parish events. From 12 to 20 youth participate in EYC on any given Sunday throughout the year, and many youth have enjoyed the meaningful Camp Nazareth experience during the summer.

Leaders promote spiritual growth through Education for Ministry (EfM), a four-year program that promotes continuing education about the Bible, church history, and theology, as well as developing a sense of personal ministry. Some parishioners participate in the recently formed EfM Wide Angle program, a one-year, flexible overview of standard topics. All Saints' offers a wide variety of adult and youth formation classes between Sunday worship services. Numerous book studies, such as the very popular Saturday morning Men's Group, take place during the year virtually or in-person to discuss a variety of spiritual topics.

Creation Care provides another example of preparing for the future. Through learning gardens, environmental education, and food recovery efforts, parishioners of all ages serve together while caring for God's creation. In 2025, volunteers contributed more than 1,300 hours and helped recover over 14,000 pounds of fresh produce for food assistance programs.

Serving Our Neighbors

Outreach & Service

Outreach is central. Parishioners serve those in need through local nonprofits, including AYA House, Cabarrus Meals on Wheels, Cabarrus Victims Assistance Network, Cooperative Christian Ministry, Habitat for Humanity Cabarrus, Lockhart Child Development Center, Salvation Army Tucker Center for Hope, and The Wesbury Apartments, engaging in mission and service that reflect a shared belief that discipleship is lived through action. These ministries are not separate from mission: they are our missions.

The spiritual heart of our church comes to life when we serve outside of the church in meaningful ways and remains intricately connected as a community that knows and cares for one another.

"Several local organizations considered community pillars originated as All Saints' ministries."

When All Saints' returned to in-person worship after the pandemic, the Vestry charged a small committee with discerning current community needs. The Koinonia Committee was formed, its Greek name meaning "a deep intimate shared life among believers, as well as active partnering in the Christian faith." The committee invited leaders of numerous Cabarrus County nonprofits to discuss their missions, activities, and needs. The Holy Spirit called our congregation to partner with AYA House and the Academic Learning Center (ALC) at Fred L. Wilson Elementary School beginning in 2024.

The "Hero in Me" mentoring program at the ALC enables All Saints' volunteers to enter the school and provide encouraging support to early elementary-aged students. The weekly tutoring program focuses on reading and phonics skills. During the 2025 academic year, nine All Saints' tutors mentored 33 first and second graders. Parishioners also provide funding for the annual book fair so all students can purchase books and the school can expand its resources.

AYA House provides transitional housing for individuals who are in the recovery stages of life after incarceration, addiction, and chronic unhoused status. The two-year program provides medical, financial, and job skills development. For All Saints', this community partnership has facilitated meaningful relationships, mentorship, and friendship. Many AYA friends now attend All Saints' worship services and support parish activities. Parishioners treasure sharing the love of God with AYA friends during fellowship gatherings, fundraisers, Bible studies, and worship services.

Two highlights of the year for many parishioners and the community are Tour de Saints, an annual charity bike ride for cyclists throughout the region, and After Dark @ All Saints, an annual fundraiser offering a wide selection of classes and speakers. Proceeds from both events are distributed to eight community-based nonprofits in Cabarrus County.

A Ministry Risk

The Lockhart Child Development Center

Taking God's church into the community has always been a part of All Saints' DNA. When our church's 40-year-old parish preschool closed at the end of the 2011 school year due to financial challenges, we did not simply mourn the loss: we asked what God might do with the space.

In early 2012, Cabarrus County Schools identified preschool-aged children with no space to serve them. Being aware of All Saints' space availability, the Cabarrus Partnership for Children recognized an opportunity. The connection was made, and the Vestry charged an All Saints' task force with exploring community preschool needs and drafting a recommendation.

What the task force discovered was sobering: each year, 300 to 400 children from economically challenged homes qualified for preschool subsidies but had nowhere to go. Community leaders educated parishioners about the profound importance of early childhood education for children born into poverty, and we could not look away.

"Our resolve was to live out the Gospel instead of merely preach it."

Questions arose quickly about finances and separation of church and state. A building inspection revealed that bringing classrooms up to code would cost $500,000, a staggering figure made weightier by an existing mortgage on the new parish hall. Yet, in March of 2013, the Vestry voted unanimously to move all reserves into a building fund so that renovations could begin and the partnership moved forward.

Parish leadership committed to full transparency: open conversations occurred, tensions were addressed directly, and space was made for the Holy Spirit to lead. All Saints' applied for and received a generous grant from The Cannon Foundation, unusual for a project still in its formative stages. In July 2013, representatives from All Saints', Cabarrus Partnership for Children, and the county school district presented a proposal to the Cabarrus County Schools Board of Education, which unanimously approved the partnership.

Named for beloved Concord pediatrician and All Saints' parishioner, the late Dr. David A. Lockhart, The Lockhart Child Development Center on the All Saints' campus became the newest facility in the Cabarrus County Schools system in fall 2013. Today, the five-star Lockhart Child Development Center serves children ages three months through five years and provides mentoring for Rowan Cabarrus Community College students studying child development. With a staff of 23 individuals, the center anticipates an enrollment of 70 children with an infant waiting list in August 2026.

The risk was real, but the reward has been transformational in providing accessible, quality, affordable early childhood development services for working families.

Walking Alongside

Pastoral Care

All Saints' regards pastoral care as a vital ministry. Parishioners are grateful that clergy are grounded in seminary education and hands-on clinical pastoral training. We deeply value their spiritual companionship combined with professional training in psychology and family systems to meet parishioners' needs in all of life's seasons. Clergy develop one-on-one relationships with parishioners through various means that are very meaningful to individuals. Our rector and deacons are available to meet with parishioners by appointment, during office hours, or by phone call. Nearly every parishioner has experienced pastoral care in some way, whether through a discussion about faith during a season of doubt, a hospital visit, a phone call during a time of bereavement, or various other ways.

"As the body of Christ, parishioners are equipped to support each other."

All Saints' maintains a robust Stephen Ministry team, which trains lay individuals to provide one-to-one, confidential emotional and spiritual support to people experiencing difficult life challenges, such as grief, divorce, job loss, or illness. All Saints' also supports a Prayer Shawl Ministry team that meets monthly to knit or crochet shawls that are later blessed and prayed over by our congregation. The shawls are stored in a cabinet in the parish Welcome Center for any member to take or give to a friend or family member in need of comfort. Lay Eucharistic visitors provide Eucharist to home-bound members. One parishioner supports pastoral care by mailing a hand-written birthday card to every member of the congregation.

Resilience

Navigating Change

Even before the pandemic, All Saints' was navigating change. In 2019, we completed a full upgrade of the lighting and audio systems in the nave, improvements designed to enhance visibility and sound, and to lay the groundwork for livestreaming. No one imagined how soon that investment would matter.

In 2020, when the pandemic hit, a task force began planning for the possibility of temporary closure. When the Diocese of North Carolina announced that all in-person worship and choir rehearsals would abruptly end, our parish had to pivot quickly. Within days, our small but mighty communications team scrambled to facilitate virtual services, choosing Facebook to livestream Morning Prayer that very first week.

"What began during a crisis became a permanent, thriving ministry."

What unfolded was remarkable. Viewership grew Sunday after Sunday, with parishioners responding to readings, prayers, and sermons through live chat, present and connected despite being apart. Facebook and eventually YouTube became All Saints' virtual sanctuary. The Holy Spirit moved powerfully through this season. Parishioners moved beyond their fears, recording themselves singing or delivering readings from their homes and gardens. Together, we assembled virtual choirs people still mention today.

That spirit of creativity peaked with two extraordinary virtual services: Advent Lessons & Carols, and a Christmas Eve service uniting Bells of All Saints', Choir of All Saints', Resurrection Choir, and guest instrumentalists, all submitted as individual recordings woven into something truly holy by our talented music/communications director.

Knowing that level of manual production was not sustainable, All Saints' accelerated plans already in motion, installing four cameras in the Nave, three in the parish hall, and building a dedicated video control room. Within months of reopening, our full broadcast system was live for both Sunday services.

Reaching Further

Technology in Ministry

Technology has been a steadily growing foundation for ministry, communication, and outreach at All Saints', and our commitment to it deepens every year.

It began with intentional investment. In 2019, All Saints' upgraded to higher-speed internet, installed new parish hall screens and projectors thanks to a grant, refreshed the bulletin and Weekly Word newsletter design, and ensured every member could remain connected across our website, Facebook, Twitter, and Instagram.

In 2021, All Saints' made a transformational investment: the installation of cameras in the nave and parish hall. The communications team set up a dedicated video control room, enabling the church to stream both Sunday services, weddings, funerals, and nonprofit partner events without placing unsustainable demands on staff.

Time, Talent, Treasure

Stewardship

All Saints' strives to take a multi-faceted, holistic, and continuous approach to stewardship as part of its daily service to God. This approach involves contributions of time, talent, and treasure, as well as wise use of resources and a commitment to creation care.

Recognizing the financial burden of debt repayment and interest, All Saints' undertook the "Set Free to Serve" campaign in 2019 with the aim of eliminating the church's debt. Motivated by the initiative, one parishioner offered a generous matching challenge gift, which the parish exceeded by nearly two-fold. Since the campaign, both increased giving and attendance have contributed to improved financial footing.

The Stewardship Committee has never communicated an annual estimate of giving goal. The financial ministry plan has been developed based on total estimates of giving and anticipated additional revenue sources. We have recognized the need to be as transparent as possible regarding the monetary needs of the church, and, to that end, the Stewardship Committee plans to pivot this year and be fully transparent about the church's financial ministry plan needs. One key goal is to support the church's full operating budget through estimates of giving instead of current dependence on other income sources, such as the child development center rent or investment income.

All Saints' approach to stewardship extends beyond money to truly valuing time, talent, and judicious use of resources. The church staff is credited with helping balance the financial ministry plan by managing expenses wisely. The parish enjoys an energetic and active Creation Care team that has enhanced the church grounds and gardens, including an annual "teaching and sharing" vegetable garden. In 2018, All Saints' was the first church in the country to co-sponsor The Arbor Day Foundation's Energy-Saving Tree Program at the local level. Stewardship truly is a holistic ministry at All Saints'.

Listening Together

Discernment & Adaptation

Discernment and adaptation are grounded in our shared commitment to grow in faith and empower others to do the same, while striving to love as God loves. Listening to the congregation's reflections, one thing becomes clear: this is a church that cares for one another, to the needs of the community, and to where God may be calling us next.

Discernment often happens in very relational and practical ways. People notice needs because they are connected through worship, small groups, friendships, and ongoing ministry involvement. That close connection to daily life in the congregation naturally extends outward into the wider community, where our church is closely linked to community partners. These relationships help our church remain grounded in what is happening around it and respond in real time to changing needs: food insecurity, housing challenges, crisis support, and care for families.

Worship and shared life play an important role in discernment. Through prayer, conversation, and congregational engagement, themes often emerge about where needs exist, where energy is present, and where our church is being invited to respond. Ours is not a top-down decision-making process; shared awareness grows out of life together.

"God of all creation, fill us with passion for justice and dignity for each neighbor in our community. Guide and support us through Koinonia to follow your example and your command to do justice, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with you. Transform us through your spirit to become conduits of your love and compassion in all that we do so we become reconciled to you and your creation. In the name of the triune God. Amen."

Parishioners pay attention to one another. They show up in hospitals, in times of grief, in moments of crisis, in times of joy and celebration, and in everyday life. That same attention extends into the community, and it shapes how the church understands what is needed and how to respond.

All Saints' has a history of not just adapting to change but responding to change as if it is part of the church's identity. Our church listens, remains connected, and moves together in compassion and purpose, seeking to faithfully live out its call to love as God loves.

A Defining Moment

Marriage Equality

Like so many parishes, All Saints' wrestled with the Episcopal Church's approval of marriage equality. The issue had been simmering for 20 years prior to the Episcopal Church's 2015 General Convention, which passed Resolution A054, authorizing two trial marriage liturgies that eliminated gender-specific language, allowing them to be used by both same-sex and opposite-sex couples. Fortunately, All Saints' rector at the time anticipated change on the horizon. During 2013 and 2014, the rector offered classes on the history of marriage and how the institution has evolved over the course of history. The rector and Vestry engaged in conversations on the meaning and significance of All Saints' core tenets of being a place where all are welcome, striving to love as God loves, and being an active, inclusive community of faith.

For some, the Episcopal Church's action was an irreparable, cataclysmic event. A few area parishioners and rectors shared their strong opposition with others and encouraged them to consider a change. In March 2015, a group of Cabarrus County residents formed a new Anglican church. All Saints' lost approximately 28 parishioners to the controversy.

For many others at All Saints', the marriage equality issue sparked a great evolution of thought on what it means to love as God loves and to welcome all people. All Saints' experienced an expansion in its theological approach to welcoming people of all sexual orientations. In comparison to many churches, All Saints' experience with the marriage equality issue was relatively conflict free thanks to the rector's leadership and preparation. Today, many LGBTQ+ parishioners share that they feel safe, welcomed, and included.

"At All Saints', all means all."
Our Next Chapter

What We Seek in a Rector

We value a leader who strives to be:

01

A Shining Light

Whose attitudes, actions, and words reflect an inspirational faith in Jesus, an empathetic love for others, and a true concern for God's creation.

02

A Compassionate Shepherd

Who is responsive to parishioners and who "shows up" in times of need.

03

A Collaborative Leader

Whose management style enables lay leaders to use their expertise in various ministries, responsive to innovative ideas and suggestions, flexible and creative but faithful to our values as a parish: welcoming, open to diversity, centered in service, committed to discerning God's will.

04

An Inspiring Communicator

Who is an attentive listener as well as an articulate speaker, who has a sense of humor, and is well grounded in the Bible and theology.

05

An Effective Administrator

Who is organized, proactive, and practical, yet visionary; firm and confident, when necessary, but also thoughtful, compassionate, and positive; a team builder who manages all kinds of resources well.

06

An Active Participant

Who engages in parish life outside of worship and who is supportive of All Saints' many community ministry partners.

We have found good communication between all parties to be essential in the collaborative style we enjoy. Vestry members serve as liaisons to ministry areas within the parish, such as Parish Life, Pastoral Care, Outreach, or Building and Grounds. The priest, wardens, and Vestry liaisons avoid micromanaging ministries but instead offer support and remain informed.

During this current season of transition, as All Saints' discerns God's will for a new rector, our Vestry has written a collect that the congregation prays each Sunday and has commissioned a diverse team of parishioners to seek guidance from the Holy Spirit and align our efforts with diocesan requirements. The Search Committee enlisted parish-wide input early through congregational meetings and continues to inform parishioners through the Weekly Word church newsletter, remaining true to our desire for comprehensive and transparent communication.
