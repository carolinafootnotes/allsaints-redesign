# Three directions. One decision.

**For the vestry — May 2026**

> We're picking a feeling, not a final pixel. Whichever direction we choose, we can refine the details. What we need today is a shared sense of how All Saints' should show up online.

See all three live: **[allsaints-redesign.nate-ernst7.workers.dev](https://allsaints-redesign.nate-ernst7.workers.dev)**

## The three directions at a glance

|  | **Option 1 — The Original** | **Option 2 — The Sanctuary** | **Option 3 — The Gathering** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feels like** | A clean refresh of what we have | A beautiful Episcopal prayer book | A community you'd like to join |
| **Leads with** | Familiar structure, clean navigation | Quiet beauty, stained glass, generous space | Real people, real moments, warm energy |
| **What it says about us** | "We're organized and up-to-date." | "We're rooted, reverent, and welcoming." | "We're alive, growing, and glad you're here." |

| **Effort to build and maintain** | Lowest | Medium | Medium |

## What each one actually looks like

### Option 1 — The Original
A multi-page website in the shape most people expect: home, visit, what's happening, get involved. A reliable, clean refresh of what we have today. Nothing surprising, nothing flashy. The familiarity is the feature.

**Pick this if:** We want the smallest possible change and the fastest path to launch.

### Option 2 — The Sanctuary
A single, scrolling page that unfolds the story of All Saints' slowly and elegantly. Large serif type, generous white space, and the stained glass as a quiet anchor. It feels like walking into the nave on a bright Sunday morning.

**Pick this if:** We want the site to reflect the beauty and depth of our worship — and we're willing to invest in good photography to carry it.

### Option 3 — The Gathering
A single, scrolling page that leads with photos of our community — people, the labyrinth, the altar, the bell tower. The burgundy and gold are bolder. The tone is warmer and more direct. It feels like the coffee hour after service.

**Pick this if:** We want newcomers to see immediately that something alive is happening here — and we're ready to lean into that story.

---

## What changes, regardless of direction

All three options share:

- The same **logo, colors, and fonts**.
- The **same service times, address, and clergy information**, presented clearly.
- **Fewer menu items** — 5–6 instead of 10+.
- **Mobile-first design** — works on a phone, which is how ~78% of newcomers will see us.
- **Real All Saints' photography** — no stock.
- **Clear paths to give, watch live, and contact us**.
- **A home for the Rector Search**, our weekly bulletin, and the Weekly Word.

The decision is about tone and structure, not about what information we include.

---

## How we'll decide

1. Each vestry member reviews the live URL before the meeting.
2. We open the meeting with a brief walk-through of each option (~10 minutes).
3. Straw poll, discussion, decision. 30 minutes total.
4. Whichever direction we pick, we refine together in the build — colors, copy, and photos are still in our hands.

---

## Recommendation: Option 2 — The Sanctuary

The Sanctuary is the strongest fit for All Saints' for three reasons:

1. **It honors what makes us specifically All Saints'.** The stained glass anchors the page. The single-scroll storytelling lets the building, the worship, and the community unfold the way they unfold in person — slowly, with room to breathe. It looks like our church, not a generic Episcopal template.

2. **It speaks to both audiences we need to serve.** Newcomers get warmth and clarity. Long-time members get dignity and beauty. The Original is too plain to spark interest; the Gathering risks reading as too casual for our parish identity. The Sanctuary lands in the middle on purpose.

3. **It's the right tone for this moment.** During a rector search, a church's website is the first answer to the question "is this a healthy parish?" The Sanctuary's calm confidence answers that question without saying a word.

**Honest trade-off:** The Sanctuary leans on photography. If we don't keep refreshing real photos of worship and community, it can start to feel empty. We mitigate this with a 90-day post-launch photo plan led by Brian (Communications) — three to five new photos per quarter, woven into the existing layouts.

If the vestry leans differently in the room, the Gathering is the credible alternative. The Original is not recommended — it's a refresh, not a step forward, and we'll be having this same conversation again in three years.
