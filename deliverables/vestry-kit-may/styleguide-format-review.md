# Styleguide Format Review — Vestry Comparison Page

**Audience:** ~12 non-technical vestry members, glancing once on laptop or phone, in or around a meeting.
**Goal:** Make three redesign directions feel different at a glance, without requiring design vocabulary.

---

## 1. Verdict

### A. Format: **A3 — Hybrid (screenshot-led, comparison row beneath).**

A1 (real fragments only) is what designers want. It is not what church volunteers need. A bare H1 in Cormorant next to a bare H1 in Inter means nothing to someone who has never thought about typefaces. They will not "feel" the difference from a single word in isolation — they need it in context.

A2 (lite brand guide) is the worst option for this audience. Swatches, type ramps, and annotated samples read as homework. Volunteers will scroll past, default to "they all look fine," and abstain. Brand-guide language signals "this is for the designer, not me."

A3 wins because the screenshot does the emotional work — a vestry member instantly sees *that one feels like a quiet chapel, that one feels like a community board* — and the small comparison row underneath confirms *why* without forcing them to study it. The screenshot earns the right to show the details.

### B. Layout: **B3 — Per-dimension rows, with a screenshot row at the top.**

Combine the two. Lead with one full-width row of three hero screenshots side-by-side (the "feel" row). Then 5–7 short rows beneath, each a single dimension across all three options. This is the only layout that lets a viewer answer the actual question: *"how is v2 different from v3, specifically?"* Side-by-side columns force vertical re-reading; stacked rows hide comparison entirely.

On mobile: collapse the three-up to a horizontal scroll-snap or a compact 3-column grid with smaller type. Don't restack to one-per-row — that destroys the comparison.

---

## 2. The 5–7 Dimensions to Include

1. **Hero screenshot** (the "feel" row — does the work)
2. **The vibe in three words** — plain-English adjectives chosen by you, not the vestry. e.g., *Familiar / Reverent / Welcoming* vs. *Quiet / Prayerful / Focused* vs. *Warm / Communal / Active*. This is the single most important addition.
3. **Color palette** — three swatches per option, named in human words ("deep wine + cream" not "#5C1F2A").
4. **Typography sample** — one short real phrase ("All are welcome at God's table") rendered in each option's actual fonts. Not "Aa Bb Cc." A real sentence carries the personality.
5. **Photography treatment** — a single representative photo from each option, same subject if possible, showing the treatment (warm/desaturated, full-bleed/contained, candid/posed).
6. **Primary button** — rendered live, with the actual CTA copy each option would use ("Plan Your Visit" vs. "Come Pray With Us" vs. "Join Us Sunday").
7. **"If you pick this, we look like…"** — one sentence per option, in plain language. This is the closer.

That's it. Seven rows. No more.

---

## 3. What to STOP / Not Include

- **No type scale, no token names, no font weights, no spacing units.** Volunteers don't vote on 16px vs. 18px.
- **No grid system, no breakpoint discussion, no component inventory.**
- **No accessibility scoring on this page.** All three should already meet AA — that's a pre-condition, not a comparison axis. Mention it once at the bottom as a baseline guarantee.
- **No "pros and cons" lists per option.** That's the existing showcase page's job. This page is sensory, not analytical.
- **No price, no timeline, no platform.** Those belong in the decision kit, not here. Mixing them muddies the visual choice.
- **Don't show the current live site as a fourth column.** It dilutes focus and pulls toward "well, the current one is fine." If it must appear, put it as a small "today" thumbnail in the page intro, not as a peer in the comparison.

---

## 4. Things to Add That You Hadn't Proposed

- **A one-line "who this is for" intro at the top.** *"Three directions. Same content, three personalities. Pick the one that feels like us."* Frames the choice as taste, not technical merit.
- **A "click to see it live" link beneath each column.** Makes the screenshot a doorway, not a dead end.
- **An emoji or single-word "feels like" tag per option** (e.g., "🕯 chapel," "📖 prayer book," "🤝 fellowship hall"). Vestry will quote these to each other in the meeting. Give them the language.
- **A closing single question, big type:** *"Which one sounds like All Saints' to you?"* No form, no vote button — just the question. Lets people sit with it.
- **Persona risk to flag:** non-techy viewers will trust the *photo* more than anything else on the page. Make sure each option's representative photo is genuinely representative, not cherry-picked. If v3's hero photo is the warmest, v3 will win on photo alone regardless of merit.

---

## 5. Success Looks Like

A vestry member opens the page on their phone in the parking lot before the meeting, scrolls once, and says out loud: *"Oh — number two feels like us."* In 30 seconds. Without ever using the word "design."
