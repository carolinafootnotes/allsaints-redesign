## Section 1: Welcome

All are welcome in this place. Whether you are visiting from afar or have lived in Concord for a long time, you are welcome here.

Wherever you are on your journey, you are welcome here. Your hopes, dreams, aspirations, sorrows, concerns, and frustrations are welcome, too. We don't ask you to have it all figured out before you walk in. We gather each Sunday to pray, to listen, to break bread, and to be sent back out — and we'd be glad to have you with us.

## Section 2: Our two services

We offer two Sunday liturgies. Both are Holy Eucharist, Rite II, from the Book of Common Prayer. Both are livestreamed on Facebook and YouTube if you'd rather join us from home first.

**9:00 AM — Parish Hall.** A less formal, family-friendly Eucharist. Dress is casual. There is often a children's sermon, and at communion the children are invited to gather around the altar with the priest. If you're visiting with kids — especially small ones — this is the easiest place to land.

**11:10 AM — in the Nave (the main Sanctuary).** Our traditional Sunday service: Rite II from the prayer book, traditional hymnody, and the Choir of All Saints' with organ. This is our most-attended service, and Episcopalians from any background tend to feel immediately at home.

Between the two, from 10:10 to 11:00 AM, we offer Christian formation for adults and for children and youth — in person and on Zoom. You're welcome to come to any of it; you're equally welcome to come for one service and head home.

## Section 3: What happens during the service

If you've never been to an Episcopal service, the shape is simple even though the language is old. A bulletin is provided when you arrive, and it walks you through everything page by page. The Book of Common Prayer is in the pew, but you won't need to hunt — the bulletin tells you when to stand, sit, kneel, or stay seated.

The liturgy moves through four movements:

- **Gathering.** We open with a hymn, a prayer, and a song of praise — settling together into worship.
- **The Word.** Readings from the Hebrew Scriptures, the Psalms, the Epistles, and the Gospel; a sermon; the Nicene Creed; and prayers for the church and the world.
- **The Holy Eucharist.** We exchange the peace, bring forward bread and wine, give thanks, and share communion at the altar.
- **The Sending.** A blessing, a final hymn, and we are sent out — "to love and serve the Lord."

The whole service runs about an hour to an hour and fifteen minutes.

## Section 4: Communion

At All Saints', all who love God and are drawn to Christ are welcome at Christ's table. You don't need to be Episcopalian, and you don't need to be a member of All Saints' — only to come forward and receive.

If you'd prefer not to receive, you are warmly invited to come forward for a blessing — simply cross your arms over your chest at the altar rail, and the priest will bless you. Gluten-free wafers are available; just ask an usher or let the priest know. [VERIFY: gluten-free wafers offered?]

If you have questions about baptism — for yourself or for a child — please reach out to the clergy. We'd love to talk with you.

## Section 5: Children

Children are a full part of worship at All Saints'. They are not a distraction; they are the church. You will hear them, and that is good news.

- **At 9:00** the service is built with families in mind. Children often come forward for a children's sermon and are invited around the altar at communion.
- **At 11:10** children are welcome in the Nave throughout the service.
- **Nursery care** is available during the morning for infants and young children, staffed by familiar faces. [VERIFY: nursery hours, age range, and exact location — live /learn/ page names staff (Katie, Jane, Kristen) but confirm current schedule.]
- **Christian formation for children** meets between services, 10:10–11:00 AM, using the "Dig-In" curriculum. Middle and high schoolers have their own gatherings during the week. [VERIFY: Sunday-morning formation rooms and ages.]

If you have a child with sensory needs or any other accommodation, please email or call ahead — we're glad to help you plan.

## Section 6: What to wear, when to arrive, and parking

**What to wear.** Whatever you're comfortable in. At the 9:00 you'll see jeans and shorts; at the 11:10 you'll see anything from khakis to a coat and tie. Neither is a uniform. Come as you are.

**When to arrive.** Plan to arrive about 10–15 minutes early so a greeter can welcome you, hand you a bulletin, point you toward the nursery or restrooms, and help you find a seat. If you're running late, come anyway — we'll find you a spot.

**Where to park.** We're at 525 Lake Concord Road NE in Concord. [VERIFY: visitor parking location and any reserved spaces — confirm with parish office; live site does not document this.] Look for the greeters at the main entrance; they'll get you to the right room.
