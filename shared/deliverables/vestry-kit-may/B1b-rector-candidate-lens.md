# B1b — Rector Candidate Lens

**Site:** allsaintsconcord.org · **Reviewed:** May 2, 2026
**Lens:** A discerning Episcopal priest considering All Saints' as a call
**Companion to:** B1-site-audit.md (don't re-litigate the 404s; here we ask what the *content* says about the parish to a candidate)

---

## 1. What a rector candidate actually looks for

A priest in discernment with a Diocesan Transition Officer (here, Canon Marion Sprott) typically reads the OTM portfolio first, then audits the parish website to triangulate. They look for:

1. **A self-aware identity statement.** Not a slogan — a sentence or two that says *who* this parish is, *what* it values, and *who it is not*. Candidates trust specifics ("Anglo-Catholic, choral, urban-edge mission") and distrust generic ("All are welcome").
2. **Liturgical practice, named.** Rite I vs. Rite II. Eucharistic frequency. Hymnal vs. Wonder, Love & Praise vs. supplemental. Daily Office. Choral tradition. This signals theology without arguing it.
3. **Clergy and lay leadership depth.** Bios with personality. Wardens and vestry named. A sense that the parish is not run by the rector alone.
4. **Active formation, outreach, and pastoral care.** Real, current programs with named leaders, not aspirational language. EfM is a strong signal of seriousness about adult formation; Stephen Ministry of seriousness about pastoral care.
5. **Children, youth, and young families.** The single hardest demographic for mainline parishes. Candidates look for evidence — not promises — of an alive program.
6. **Honest financial and transition signals.** Recent annual report, a current giving page, a clean rector-search section with timeline, search committee names, and a public link to the parish profile when ready.
7. **Community and diocesan posture.** What does this parish *do* in Concord? What's the relationship with the Diocese of NC? Any partnerships (Lockhart CDC is the obvious one here)?
8. **A sense the website is cared for.** Candidates extrapolate: if the homepage carries placeholder text and a 3-year-old stewardship appeal, they assume the same neglect runs through committees, finances, and pastoral follow-up.

## 2. What's currently strong (a candidate would find reassuring)

- **The clergy page is genuinely good.** Four full bios with education, ordination history, prior cures, families, hobbies. Bernacki's path (RC → Episcopal, 1988), Reese's second-career trajectory (dietician → priest, VTS '08), Cahoon's diaconal focus on nursing-home pastoral care, Johanssen's interim-ministry training and VTS M.Div. — all of this reads as a real, multi-vocational team. Two VTS grads on the bench is itself a signal.
- **Worship content names actual practice.** Both Sunday services are Rite II; the 11:10 is described as "standard Rite II prayer book service with traditional hymnody and choir with organ"; the 9:00 is family-friendly with children invited to gather at the altar. That is enough liturgical specificity for a candidate to picture themselves presiding.
- **Music program has unusual depth for a parish this size.** Five ensembles named: Choir of All Saints' (Anglican choral tradition, Wed 7pm), Resurrection Choir (Sun 8:15am rehearsal), Bells of All Saints' (handbells), St. Nicholas Choir (grades 1–3, Orff-Schulwerk), Junior Choir (grades 4–7, Orff-Schulwerk). Orff-Schulwerk for two children's choirs is a real investment, not lip service.
- **Daily Office is in the rhythm.** Tuesday 8pm Compline (Bernacki) and Wednesday noon Noonday Prayer, both online. Candidates read this as "this parish prays daily, not just Sundays."
- **Adult formation exists and is named.** Two EfM groups under trained mentors (Sue Gelarden, Dave Millar), Cursillo connection, weekly Adult Christian Formation 10:10–11:00 in person and on Zoom.
- **Pastoral care is concrete.** Stephen Ministry, Stitches for the Saints (third Tuesday, prayer shawls), meal ministry with a freezer, eucharistic visitors. Real, named, ongoing.
- **History page is a strong founding narrative.** 1877 first baptism (Elizabeth C. Gibson), 1893 diocesan recognition, the 1969 Concord/Kannapolis merger, the 1972 first service on the current campus, 1984 Governor's Volunteer Award, 2014 Lockhart CDC opening. A candidate reads this as "this parish has a story it can tell about itself."
- **Vestry roster is published with named officers and liaison areas.** Chuck Collier (Sr. Warden), Andrea Holdren (Jr. Warden), Victor Clark (Treasurer), Kim Gordick (Clerk), and 10 liaisons. A candidate can immediately see which lay leader covers Worship (Brantley), Outreach (Davis), Adult Formation (Ward-Bowman), Youth Formation (Wagner), Pastoral Care (Bahn), etc.
- **Lockhart CDC is a tangible community footprint.** Owning a children's developmental center on campus (since 2014) is a real, concrete answer to "what does this parish do for Concord?"
- **The parish is in motion right now.** April 16 rector-search update, April 19 and April 22 congregational input meetings convened by Canon Sprott to build the parish portfolio, 2025 Annual Report posted in January, May iconography retreat. A candidate browsing in May 2026 sees a parish that is awake.

## 3. What's currently weak or missing (would actively hurt our standing)

- **No identity statement that survives a candidate's first read.** "Disciples Making Disciples for Jesus" / "Loves Like God Loves" / "All Are Welcome" is a vision-mission-tagline triple that says almost nothing distinguishing. A candidate cannot tell from this whether All Saints' is broad-church mainstream Episcopal, evangelical-leaning, progressive, contemplative, or charismatic. The /welcome-from-the-rector/ page is unsigned and generic — it should be either Johanssen's interim voice or removed.
- **No "What to expect on Sunday" piece.** Service times are listed; no narrative of what walking in is like — vesture, length, communion practice (open table?), what to wear, where to park, where children go. This is the single most-requested page on a parish site by both visitors and candidates.
- **Rector search section is fragmented and partly broken.** The top-nav `/rector-search-committee/` is 404 (per B1). Active content lives at `/next-steps-and-sharing-your-responses/` (a blog post) and `/your-perspectives-are-needed/` (April 16 update). There is **no public search-committee page**: no roster, no chair, no timeline graphic, no link to the parish profile, no "candidates: contact X." Canon Sprott is named only in passing. For a parish actively recruiting, this is the most consequential gap.
- **No public parish profile / OTM portfolio link.** The April 22 update explicitly says the portfolio "will be posted on the All Saints' website and published throughout the Episcopal Church." As of this review it is not yet there. Once it exists, it has to live one click from the homepage.
- **Outreach page reads dated and thin.** /serve/ leads with Episcopal Relief & Development, Nets for Life, Syrian refugees, Haiti — language with a 2015–2018 feel. The "Gardens" and "Events Planning" sections still describe ER&D instead of those ministries (per B1). A candidate looking for current local mission posture finds Lockhart CDC mentioned only in passing here.
- **Children, youth, and young families story is buried.** The /learn/ page actually has good content (nursery staff named, "Dig-In" curriculum, Breakfast with Jesus middle-school study at the parish, Coffee with Jesus high-school at Panera, EYC Sundays 5:30–7:30). This is a real youth program. But it is hidden three clicks deep behind a 404 dropdown parent. A candidate scanning for "do you have kids?" may not find it.
- **Giving page is a 2022 deficit appeal** (per B1). For a candidate, this reads as either financial fragility or content neglect — either is bad.
- **History timeline ends at 2014.** Nothing on the prior rector's tenure, retirement (Nov 2025), or Johanssen's interim arrival. A candidate wants to know *what just happened here*.
- **No diocesan posture.** No mention of the Diocese of NC beyond Canon Sprott in one update post. No bishop reference, no convocation, no diocesan ministries the parish participates in. Candidates orient by diocese.
- **No sermons / preaching samples.** Candidates want to hear what's preached here. Services are livestreamed to Facebook/YouTube but there is no sermon archive on the site.
- **Photography is mixed-vintage.** Fellowship and Play pages carry 2017-era photos (per B1). Stephen Ministry's commissioning photo is from June 2024 (appropriate). A candidate notices when the *people* in the photos look like a different decade than the *posts*.
- **Maintenance bugs** (placeholder footer text, broken Facebook widget, two "Bio coming soon" staff entries) signal neglect on a site a candidate is treating as a primary information source.

## 5. Real content that signals "we are an active, healthy Episcopal parish"

A candidate scanning quickly should encounter, in roughly this order, signals that this is a real Episcopal parish in motion:

1. **Two Sunday Eucharists, both Rite II, one contemporary and one choral-traditional, both livestreamed.** This is a healthy modern Episcopal pattern.
2. **Daily Office twice a week, online.** Bernacki praying Compline on Tuesday nights is a small, telling fact.
3. **Five named music ensembles**, including two children's choirs using Orff-Schulwerk. Few parishes this size invest like this.
4. **EfM with two groups and trained mentors.** Adult formation is real here.
5. **Stephen Ministry + Stitches + Meal Ministry + Eucharistic Visitors.** A complete pastoral-care stack.
6. **Lockhart CDC**, an on-campus children's center the parish opened in 2014. A tangible long-term outreach commitment.
7. **A vestry with 10 named liaison areas and active officers** — the parish is run by lay leaders, not just clergy.
8. **Active rector-search communication in April 2026** with congregational input meetings convened by the Diocese's Transitional Ministry Officer.
9. **An annual report posted publicly** (2025, embedded January 2026).

These are the points that, sequenced and surfaced, change a candidate's impression from "small Southern parish in transition" to "real diocesan partner with a serious worship and formation life."

## 6. Gaps the redesigns must address (content/IA, not visual)

Each of the three design options needs to carry the following content slots, regardless of visual treatment:

1. **A real "Who we are" statement.** Not the slogan stack. Two to four sentences naming: liturgical tradition (broad-church Episcopal, Rite II, choral and contemporary side-by-side), theological posture (Book of Common Prayer, Bible, sacrament-centered), community posture (Lockhart CDC, outreach in Concord), and current moment (in interim with Johanssen, in active rector search). Drafting this is a vestry/clergy task, not a design task.
2. **A "What to Expect on Sunday" page.** What the two services feel like, communion practice (open table, all the baptized welcome — confirm with clergy), children's participation, parking, dress, where to find a greeter. Single most-asked-for page; currently absent.
3. **A real Rector Search landing page**, not a blog post. Must include: where we are in the process, search committee chair and members, Canon Sprott's role, timeline with milestones, link to the parish profile (when posted), how parishioners give input, how candidates make contact. This page should exist as a permanent location and update through the search.
4. **The parish profile / OTM portfolio**, hosted on the site (PDF or embedded) one click from the homepage, the moment it is finalized.
5. **A current Giving page.** Hard kill on the 2022 deficit appeal. New page covers: 2026 pledge campaign, online giving, planned giving, and a one-line honest financial-health statement. (Consult treasurer Victor Clark before drafting.)
6. **A "Children, Youth & Families" page that surfaces the existing program.** Nursery, Sunday school with "Dig-In", Breakfast with Jesus, Coffee with Jesus, EYC. This content already exists; it just needs to be one click from the homepage rather than three.
7. **A "Music & Liturgy" page that names the five ensembles.** The music program is a recruiting asset both for parishioners and for a candidate; do not bury it inside /worship/ as a paragraph.
8. **A History page extended through 2026.** The 1877–2014 narrative is good. Add a paragraph covering the prior rector's tenure and retirement, Johanssen's January 2026 arrival, and the search now underway. Honest, brief.
9. **A diocesan-relationship line.** "All Saints' is a parish of the Episcopal Diocese of North Carolina; our bishop is [name]" with a link. One sentence; currently missing.
10. **Sermon archive or preaching samples.** Even a handful of Johanssen + Bernacki + Reese sermons (audio or text) would let a candidate hear the parish.
11. **Updated photography** of actual current parish life — at minimum, swap the 2017-vintage fellowship shots and refresh nothing-older-than-2024 on the homepage.
12. **Refreshed Outreach page.** Lead with current local commitments (Lockhart CDC, Cabarrus County partnerships), then named diocesan/global. Drop or factually update the Syria/Haiti/Nets-for-Life trio if they are no longer active.
