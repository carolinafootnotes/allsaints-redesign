# Episcopal Terminology Reference

A practical vocabulary guide for All Saints' Episcopal Church (Concord, NC) website copy. Use this to spot the handful of places where everyday English drifts off-tone for an Episcopal parish, and to swap in language that reads true.

Sources: The Episcopal Church (episcopalchurch.org), Episcopal Diocese of North Carolina (episdionc.org), Book of Common Prayer (1979).

---

## 1. Always-correct terms (everyday English → Episcopal usage)

| Common word | Use on the site |
|---|---|
| pastor | **priest**, **rector** (the priest in charge of a parish), or **clergy** |
| head pastor / lead pastor | **rector** |
| associate pastor | **associate priest**, **assisting priest**, or **curate** |
| youth pastor | **youth minister** or **director of youth formation** |
| church staff | **clergy and staff** (distinguish ordained from lay) |
| service | "Service times" is fine in nav; in body copy prefer **liturgy**, **Holy Eucharist**, **Morning Prayer**, etc. |
| Sunday service | **Sunday Eucharist** or **Sunday liturgy** (or just "Sunday worship") |
| communion | **Holy Eucharist** (formal), **Holy Communion** (warm/casual). "The Eucharist" is shorthand. |
| the Lord's supper | **the Eucharist** / **the Lord's table** |
| sermon | **sermon** (fine); **homily** is also common, especially for shorter weekday reflections |
| altar | **altar** or **the Lord's table** / **the holy table** |
| sanctuary (the room) | **nave** (where the congregation sits), **chancel** (around the altar), **sanctuary** (the altar area itself). "Sanctuary" colloquially is OK. |
| church members | **parishioners**, or **the people of All Saints'**; "members" is fine in membership contexts |
| congregation | **congregation** or **parish** |
| bible study | **bible study** (fine); **Christian formation**, **adult formation**, or **EfM** (Education for Ministry) for deeper programs |
| Sunday school | **children's formation**, **Christian formation for children**, or **church school** |
| small groups | **small groups** is fine; also **formation groups** |
| baptism class | **baptismal preparation** |
| getting saved / accepting Christ | **baptism**, **confirmation**, **being received into the Episcopal Church** |
| worship leader / praise band | **choir**, **organist and choirmaster**, **music minister**, **cantor** |
| volunteer team | **ministry** (e.g., Altar Guild, lay readers, lectors, chalice bearers, ushers, vergers) |
| greeters | **ushers** or **greeters** (both fine) |
| giving / tithes | **stewardship**, **giving**, or **pledge** (for annual commitment) |
| outreach | **outreach** (fine); also **mission** or **service** |
| prayer team | **intercessors** or **prayer ministry** |
| church calendar | **the liturgical calendar** / **the church year** (Advent, Christmas, Epiphany, Lent, Easter, Pentecost, Ordinary Time) |
| pastor's message | **rector's letter** / **from the rector** |
| beliefs / statement of faith | **what we believe**, often grounded in **the Nicene Creed**, **the Apostles' Creed**, and **the Book of Common Prayer** |

## 2. Phrasings that read true (Episcopal voice)

Use these as touchstones — they signal Episcopal identity without trying too hard.

- "All are welcome at the Lord's table."
- "Rooted in scripture, tradition, and reason." (the three-legged stool)
- "Common prayer" / "We pray together from the Book of Common Prayer."
- "The via media" — the middle way between Catholic and Protestant traditions.
- "We seek and serve Christ in all persons." (from the Baptismal Covenant)
- "Respect the dignity of every human being." (also Baptismal Covenant)
- "Formation for all ages."
- "A community of worship, formation, and service."
- "Whoever you are, wherever you are on your journey of faith, you are welcome here." (widely-used Episcopal welcome)
- "Liturgy, sacrament, and song."
- "The beloved community." (used frequently in the Diocese of NC)

## 3. Phrasings to avoid (off-tone for Episcopal)

These read evangelical, non-denominational, or generically Christian-marketing in a way that doesn't fit:

- "Personal Lord and Savior" / "ask Jesus into your heart"
- "Get saved" / "born again" / "altar call"
- "Doing church" / "doing life together"
- "Pastor [Name] welcomes you…" (use **The Rev. [Name]**, **The Rev. Dr. [Name]**, or **Mother/Father [First Name]** if the priest uses that style)
- "Praise team" / "worship experience" / "worship set"
- "Statement of faith" (use "What we believe" instead)
- "Bible-believing" / "bible-based" (Episcopalians read scripture seriously but don't self-describe this way)
- "On fire for God" / "radical faith" / "sold out for Jesus"
- "Campus" (for one building) — say **parish**, **church**, or **the church**
- "Service" used as a verb ("come service with us")
- "Auditorium" — say **nave** or **church**
- Heavy exclamation points and "!!" enthusiasm; Episcopal voice is warm but composed.

## 4. Diocese of North Carolina specifics

- **Diocesan Bishop:** The Rt. Rev. Sam Rodman (XII Bishop of North Carolina). On first reference: **The Rt. Rev. Samuel S. Rodman III**; thereafter **Bishop Rodman**.
- **Assistant Bishop:** The Rt. Rev. Jennifer Brooke-Davidson. First reference **The Rt. Rev. Jennifer Brooke-Davidson**; thereafter **Bishop Brooke-Davidson**.
- **Diocese name:** **The Episcopal Diocese of North Carolina** (covers central NC; Concord is in this diocese, not the Diocese of Western NC or East Carolina).
- **Diocesan framing:** "Growing into God's vision of the beloved community," "rooted in ancient faith and committed to inclusivity," "celebrating the dignity of every person." These phrases are safe to echo.
- **Five missional priorities** (good language to align with): racial reckoning, justice and healing; congregational vitality; discipleship and formation; collaboration and new communities; creation care.
- **Regional grouping:** The diocese is organized into **convocations** (not deaneries). Concord sits in the **Charlotte Convocation**. Confirm before publishing.
- **Presiding Bishop** (national, if ever referenced): **The Most Rev. Sean Rowe**.
- **Honorifics quick reference:** Priest = *The Rev.* — Bishop = *The Rt. Rev.* — Presiding Bishop = *The Most Rev.* — Deacon = *The Rev.* (or *The Rev. Deacon*).

## 5. Tone notes

The Episcopal voice is:

- **Thoughtful, not preachy.** We invite; we don't insist. We trust readers to bring their own questions.
- **Reverent, not stiff.** Comfortable with mystery, ritual, beauty, silence. Not embarrassed by old language; not afraid of plain modern language either.
- **Hospitable, not transactional.** No "first-time guest" funnels, no urgency tactics, no countdown timers to a "decision."
- **Intellectually serious.** Scripture, tradition, and reason all matter. We read books. We sit with hard questions. We don't pretend faith is simple.
- **Sacramental.** The physical world — bread, wine, water, oil, candles, bodies, neighborhoods — is where God shows up. Copy can lean into the tangible.
- **Quietly confident in identity.** Don't oversell "we're not like other churches." Just be Episcopal: liturgy, common prayer, the table open to all, formation across a lifetime, service to neighbor.

When in doubt: warmer than corporate, more grounded than enthusiastic, more invitation than instruction.
