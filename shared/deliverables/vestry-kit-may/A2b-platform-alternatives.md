# Platform alternatives — why Squarespace over Framer, Webflow, or church-specific platforms

**Companion to A2.** If someone asks "did you consider other platforms?" — yes. Here's the honest comparison.

---

## The four realistic options

### 1. Squarespace Business *(recommended)*
The safe, boring, correct choice for a 148-year parish with volunteer staff.

### 2. Framer
The design-forward alternative. Newer platform; stronger output aesthetic.

### 3. Webflow
The most powerful design-oriented web platform. Overkill for our scope.

### 4. Church-specific platforms (Subsplash, Tithe.ly Sites, Church.dev)
Built-for-churches, bundled with giving + streaming + app. Notable but eliminated below.

---

## Decision criteria (in priority order)

1. **Can non-technical staff maintain it?** Ruth Brown updates the bulletin every Friday. Brian posts new events. This is the most common operation on the site; it has to be frictionless.
2. **Does it render well on a phone?** ~78% of first-time visitors are on a phone.
3. **Does it integrate cleanly with tools we're keeping?** Realm (giving), Constant Contact (newsletter), Facebook + YouTube (livestream), SignUpGenius (events).
4. **Does it still work in 5 years?** The platform itself needs to be around.
5. **What happens if Nate steps back?** Can we find someone else to help without a specialist search?
6. **Cost, both first year and ongoing.**

## Where each platform is the right answer

- **Squarespace is right** when the goal is "a site that works for the next 5 years, maintained by volunteers, at a low cost, with proven integrations." That's All Saints'.
- **Framer is right** when the goal is "a design-forward site where the visual impression is the product, maintained by a designer." It's where a tech startup or a design studio would land today.
- **Webflow is right** when the goal is "pixel-perfect custom design with a content management system and heavy developer collaboration." Overkill for All Saints'.
- **Church-specific platforms are right** when the goal is "bundle our website, giving, app, and streaming under one roof and write one check per month." Real tradeoff: cost, lock-in, and aesthetic.

---

## Why church-specific platforms are eliminated

Subsplash, Tithe.ly Sites, Church.dev, Faithlife — all exist, all are real options for churches. We're passing on them because:

1. **Cost** — Subsplash starts at $1,200–$2,500/year and goes up. For a small parish that's already happy with Realm (giving) and Constant Contact (newsletter), we're paying for bundled tools we don't need.
2. **Aesthetic** — Their template libraries lean hard toward non-denominational / evangelical megachurch design language. Getting an Episcopal tone out of them is fighting the platform.
3. **Lock-in** — Migrating giving history off Realm to a bundled giving platform is a real operational risk. We don't need to take it.
4. **Integration direction** — We want fewer platforms with sharper responsibilities, not one platform that "does everything." OnRealm is the giving system of record; we want that to stay true.

If the church ever evaluates moving giving off Realm, the church-platform question reopens. Not today.

## Why Framer is the closest call — and we're still choosing Squarespace

Framer deserves a real answer, because:

- The output is genuinely beautiful and mobile-native in a way Squarespace has to be coaxed into matching.
- Nate, as a UX designer, would learn Framer faster than almost anyone.
- The performance is excellent — static rendering by default.
- The pricing is competitive (~$180/yr Basic).

**Why we're still choosing Squarespace:**

1. **The weekly bulletin link.** Someone besides Nate updates that file every Friday. On Squarespace, that's a click, a paste, and a save. On Framer, it involves the CMS editor or a draft-and-publish flow that's unfamiliar to non-designers. Multiply that friction by 52 weeks a year and many years.

2. **The redirect and URL-management story.** Squarespace handles 301 redirects and URL mappings in a simple settings panel. Framer's URL management is improving but is historically one of its rougher edges. We have ~30 old-site URLs that need redirects; we want that to be a non-event.

3. **The freelancer market.** If Nate steps back in 18 months, a Squarespace freelancer is a Google search away and will quote $500–$1,000 for a small update. A Framer freelancer is harder to find and typically charges more.

4. **Platform maturity for community organizations.** Framer's CMS capabilities date from 2022 (with the "Framer Sites" pivot). Squarespace has been hosting churches since before some of the vestry's grandchildren were born. For a parish planning on a 3-to-5-year horizon, the more boring platform has fewer failure modes.

5. **The aesthetic gap is closable.** The v2 ("Sanctuary") design in our mockups is achievable on Squarespace with custom CSS and careful section design. It won't be as frictionless as it would be on Framer, but it will be very close — and the final 10% of design polish is not worth the other tradeoffs.

---

## What would make us switch to Framer

For transparency, these are the signals that would make Framer the better choice:

- If Nate commits to being the primary content editor for the life of the site (not just the build).
- If a specific "design statement" is what the vestry wants, and we're willing to pay for that with weekly maintenance friction.
- If a design-oriented volunteer or staff member steps forward who'd prefer Framer's editor.
- If the budget includes ~$500/year for occasional Framer freelance help when Nate is unavailable.

None of those are true today. If any become true, we can revisit.

---

## Plain-English recommendation

Squarespace is the choice you make when you want the website to be a small, reliable thing that a parish can keep up without thinking about it. Framer is the choice you make when you want the website to be a design statement. All Saints' needs the first thing. We can ship a beautiful site on Squarespace — the three directions we've designed all prove that.
