# C1 — Rollout narrative

**Purpose:** The story we tell the congregation about the website change. The backbone every other piece of communication hangs on — parish email, bulletin blurb, pulpit announcement, FAQ, social posts, launch email.

If someone asks the question, this is the answer.

---

## The story in three sentences

> Our website is how most people meet All Saints' for the first time — and it's been quietly getting in our own way. We're moving to a simpler, more welcoming site this summer that works on a phone, is easier for our staff to update, and costs us less to maintain. Nothing about who we are is changing; just the front door.

---

## Why now

We've been on WordPress for years. It works, but it's slow to update and increasingly fragile. About a third of what the menu points to is already broken. Meanwhile, most people who first encounter All Saints' do so on a phone — and our site wasn't built with that in mind. The redesign is a small, overdue correction.

It's also the right moment. During a rector search, a church's website is its first answer to the question *is this a healthy parish?* We want that answer to be *yes* — clearly and quickly.

## What's changing

- **A cleaner, friendlier home.** Fewer menu items. Clearer paths to Sunday services, giving, and the livestream. Mobile-first — built for a phone.
- **Real All Saints' photography.** The stained glass, the nave, the labyrinth, our people. Nothing stock.
- **A platform that's easier to maintain.** Staff can update service times, bulletins, and event sign-ups themselves, without waiting on a developer.
- **A modest cost reduction.** Roughly cost-neutral with what WordPress costs today, but we shed the plugin maintenance burden and the security exposure.

## What isn't changing

- The URL — allsaintsconcord.org stays.
- Our **mission and vision**. Still disciples making disciples. Still loving like God loves.
- Our **worship** — service times, order, livestream links, all the same.
- **Giving** stays on Realm. Your giving history, recurring gifts, and pledges are untouched.
- The **member portal** (Realm) stays where it is.
- The **Weekly Word newsletter** (Constant Contact) stays where it is.
- **Lockhart CDC's website** stays separate at lockhartcdc.org.
- Anything you've bookmarked will redirect to the new location for at least 30 days.

## How we're bringing the congregation along

1. **Announce early.** 3–4 weeks before launch, we mention it in bulletins, the Weekly Word, and from the pulpit.
2. **Preview before launch.** The vestry and clergy see and approve the staging site first. Then the congregation gets a two-week window to click around and flag anything confusing or broken before it goes public.
3. **Launch with a welcome.** When the new site is live, a parish email walks through what's new and where to find the things people use most.
4. **Keep the door open.** A simple feedback form stays active after launch for anyone who can't find something or has an idea.

## What we're asking of the congregation

Nothing, until the preview window. At that point, about two weeks before launch, we'll ask anyone interested to click through the new site and tell us what they can't find or what feels off. That's it.

## Tone guidance for all downstream comms

- **Warm, direct, honest.** Not corporate. Not defensive.
- **Respect the long-time member.** They've been here longer than the website has. Acknowledge that change is uncomfortable and promise continuity where it's real.
- **Respect the newcomer.** They haven't been here at all yet. The redesign is largely for them — say so.
- **Self-deprecate when true.** It's OK to say "yes, some of the menu was broken; we knew; we're fixing it."
- **Don't oversell.** This is a website, not a reformation. Small, competent, and done is the goal.
- **Episcopal voice.** Liturgical rhythm is welcome; jargon isn't.

## Anti-patterns (don't do this)

- Don't call it a "re-brand." We're not re-branding. We're re-plumbing.
- Don't frame it as "bringing All Saints' into the digital age." Patronizing.
- Don't over-claim. If the Weekly Word open rate goes up, great — but we're not doing this for metrics we haven't been tracking.
- Don't blame WordPress in public. No need to embarrass a platform that served us fine for a while.
