Three directions. One decision.

We're picking a feeling, not a final pixel. Whichever direction we choose, we can refine the details. What we need today is a shared sense of how All Saints' should show up online.

What changes, regardless

All three options share the same logo, colors, and fonts; the same service times, address, and clergy; fewer menu items (5–6 instead of 10+); mobile-first design; real All Saints' photography; clear paths to give, watch live, and contact us; and a home for the Rector Search, weekly bulletin, and the Weekly Word. The decision is about tone and structure, not what information we include.

A leaning, not a verdict

All three are credible. Each says something true about us. The right answer is the one this vestry can stand behind together — there is no wrong choice in this room.

Where opinion has landed so far. When these were shown earlier, several members favored Option 1 (The Original) for its familiarity and low maintenance — a meaningful signal we should not dismiss. Others were drawn to Option 2 (The Sanctuary) for its quieter, more reverent tone.

If we don't reach consensus, my lean is Option 2 — The Sanctuary, for one reason worth saying plainly: a website is the first thing a curious newcomer or a discerning rector candidate sees. The Sanctuary's calm beauty answers the question "is this a healthy parish?" without saying a word, which matters during this in-between season.

The honest case for Option 1. It's the lowest-effort option to maintain, the most familiar to long-time members, and it carries the least risk if photography lags. If the vestry's collective gut is here, that's a defensible call.

The honest trade-off either way. Sanctuary leans on photography; Brian (Communications) will lead a 90-day photo refresh after launch. Original is safer but plainer — we trade interest for ease.

How we'll decide

  1. Each member reviews the live URL before the meeting.
  2. Brief walk-through of each option (~10 minutes).
  3. Straw poll, discussion, decision. 30 minutes total.
  4. Whichever direction we pick, colors, copy, and photos remain in our hands during the build.

II · Platform

Why Squarespace.

We're not picking a website. We're picking what the office and staff will live with for the next 5+ years. The platform decision is bigger than the design.

Recommendation

Squarespace, Business plan, billed annually with the nonprofit discount.

~$248 the first year, ~$276/year after that. All-in. No add-ons to chase, no specialist to hire.

Why this matters in plain terms

Our current site quietly creates work for whoever maintains it — small breakages, software pieces that need updating, a structure only one person fully understands. None of that is visible from the pew, but it adds up to hours and risk every month.

Squarespace is the all-in-one alternative most parishes our size land on. One bill, one login, one place to learn. If the person who maintains the site changes, the next person can be up to speed in an afternoon — not a search for a specialist.

The cost picture

Net: roughly cost-neutral compared to today — but we shed the ongoing maintenance burden and the risk of being one volunteer away from a stuck site.

Who handles the build

I'll handle it. Build, content, images, and the small technical pieces (giving widget, livestream, map, contact form) are all in scope and accounted for. Vestry doesn't need to source a developer or budget for one. If you ever want a freelancer to take over later, Squarespace makes that straightforward — there's a published directory of them.

III · Sign-ups

Sign-up platform — recommendation.

Today, parish sign-ups (Easter Flowers, Maundy Thursday Dinner, and the rest) live in scattered, ad-hoc pages. The new site needs one consistent place for them.

Recommendation

Use SignUpGenius. Start on the free tier. Upgrade to Starter ($8.99/mo, ~$108/year) only if we want custom branding.

The new website links out to SignUpGenius for any event with a sign-up. One pattern, one place to look, no surprises.

Why SignUpGenius

  • It's the standard for churches and schools. Most parishioners have used it before — no learning curve.
  • The free tier is genuinely usable. Unlimited sign-ups, unlimited participants, email reminders included.
  • Built for the right job. Designed for slot-based coordination (bring a side dish, sign up for a vigil hour) — not just yes/no RSVPs.

How it shows up on the new site

One "Events & Sign-ups" page. Each event is a card with a date, a short description, and a "Sign up" button. When a sign-up closes, the card retires itself. This replaces the current "Sign Ups" menu, which today carries five seasonal entries that quickly go stale.

Whoever currently posts events on our site will continue to do so — the workflow is simpler, not harder.

What we need to decide today

One thing: approval to standardize on SignUpGenius for parish sign-ups going forward. Cost: $0. Risk: low. Reversible: yes.
