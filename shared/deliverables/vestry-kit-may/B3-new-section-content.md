# 1. Rector Search (`/about/rector-search`)

**Purpose:** The single, consolidated place where the parish — members and visitors alike — can see where we are in the search, who's on the committee, and how to contribute. Replaces the currently broken `/rector-search-committee/` URL and the blog-post stand-in at `/next-steps-and-sharing-your-responses/`.

**Design principle:** Honest, confident, and dated. Our vulnerability during this season is a strength, not something to hide.

**Lifecycle:** Lives until the new rector is called. Retires gracefully — its URL redirects to `/about/clergy` and Fr. John (or his successor) writes a reflective closing post for `/news`.

---

| Section | Content type | Who updates |

## Notes for the editor

- **The "Last updated" stamp is load-bearing.** People need to know they're reading current information. Update it any time the Status or Timeline changes — even small wording tweaks.
- **Tone:** confident and honest. "We don't know yet when we'll call our next rector" is perfectly fine to say. What we want to avoid is either empty reassurance ("everything is going smoothly!") or anxious over-explanation.
- **Privacy for committee members:** some may prefer not to be publicly named. Lead with the roster but allow opt-outs; show headcount + roles if needed.
- **When a call is made:** replace the Status section with a brief celebration note, leave the Timeline as historical record, and begin the redirect to `/about/clergy` ~30 days after the new rector's start date. Write a reflective post for `/news` closing the chapter.

# 2. Events & Sign-ups (`/news/events` or `/events`)

**Purpose:** The single home for every parish event with a sign-up, replacing the currently broken "Sign Ups" top-level nav and the scattered pages that result (Personal Communion stuck at 2021, Easter Flowers 404'd).

**Design principle:** Each event is a self-contained card. Adding a new event is duplicating a card. Removing one is deleting a card. No workflows, no dropdowns.

**Lifecycle:** A working page. Events come and go continuously. Anyone on staff with 30 minutes of Squarespace training can maintain it.

---

## Category tags to use

Keep the set small — too many categories and nothing feels categorized. Recommended:

- **SPECIAL SERVICE** — Holy Week, Feast Days, baptisms open to the parish, etc.
- **CLASS / FORMATION** — Adult formation, EFM, Sunday School special sessions
- **OUTREACH** — Food drives, CCM events, service projects
- **FELLOWSHIP** — Dinners, socials, small groups
- **RETREAT** — Multi-day immersions
- **PRAYER** — Weekday services, vigils
- **MEMORIAL** — Flower dedications, saints-commemoration events

---

## Maintenance pattern

**Weekly (Ruth Brown or communications volunteer):**
- Remove any cards whose event has passed.
- Ensure all active cards have working sign-up links.

**Per event (anyone posting a new event):**
1. In Squarespace editor, duplicate an existing card.
2. Update: date, title, 1–2 sentence description, category tag, link.
3. Save. That's it.

**Quarterly (Communications Director / Brian):**
- Review upcoming-events load. If <3 events are showing, prompt ministry leaders for what's coming.

---

## Why not embed SignUpGenius directly?

SignUpGenius does offer embed codes. We're choosing to **link out** instead because:

1. Embedded sign-up widgets often break on mobile.
2. Members recognize the SignUpGenius interface from other churches and schools — opening it in its own tab maintains that trust.
3. If we ever change sign-up platforms, we don't have to touch the website's layout.

If the vestry later prefers embedded widgets for certain high-profile events (e.g., Easter Flowers), Squarespace Business's Code Injection supports it.
