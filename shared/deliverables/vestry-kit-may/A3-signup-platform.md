# Sign-up platform — recommendation

**For the vestry — May 2026**

> The current site uses ad-hoc WordPress pages for sign-ups (Easter Flowers, Maundy Thursday Dinner, etc.). The new site needs one consistent way to coordinate these — easy for staff to set up, easy for parishioners to use.

## Recommendation

**Use SignUpGenius. Start on the free tier. Upgrade to Starter ($8.99/mo) only if we want custom branding.**

The new website links out to SignUpGenius for any event with a sign-up sheet. One pattern, one place to look, no surprises.

## Why SignUpGenius

- **It's the standard for churches and schools.** Most parishioners have used it before — no learning curve.
- **The free tier is genuinely usable.** Unlimited sign-ups, unlimited participants, email reminders included.
- **Built for the right job.** Designed specifically for slot-based coordination (bring a side dish, sign up for a vigil hour, volunteer for a shift) — not just RSVPs.
- **No platform lock-in.** Lives outside the website; if we ever change anything, sign-ups are unaffected.

## What we considered and didn't pick

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Google Forms** | Free, but the UX is utilitarian. No slot-based sign-ups, no reminders, no "the chicken side is full" logic. Fine for surveys, wrong shape for events. |
| **Eventbrite** | Polished, but built for ticketed events. Charges a fee per registration even for free events on most plans. Overkill for a parish dinner. |
| **Squarespace native forms** | Comes with our website plan. Too basic — doesn't handle multi-slot sign-ups or reminders. We'd outgrow it within a season. |

## How it shows up on the new site

A single "Events & Sign-ups" page. Each event is a card with a date, a one-line description, and a "Sign up" button that opens SignUpGenius in a new tab. When a sign-up closes, the card archives automatically (or the staff member who posted it removes it).

This replaces the current "Sign Ups" top-level menu item, which today contains 5+ seasonal entries that quickly go stale.
